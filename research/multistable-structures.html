      Multistable Structures

      Vision

      We design structures with several stable shapes and study how to switch between them through elastic instability. A curved shell is a simple example. Once the applied load reaches a threshold, the shell can snap into another configuration and remain there after the load is removed.

      We combine theory, simulation and experiments to understand how folding, curvature and local stiffness set these thresholds. This helps us choose the shapes a structure can hold and the stimuli that trigger a transition.

      Why it matters

      Snap-through often limits the working range of a conventional structure. A deliberately designed transition can release stored energy, guide motion or provide a well-defined mechanical state.

      Because each shape can persist without continuous actuation, geometry can carry part of the work usually assigned to motors or controls. Repeatability matters, especially when a device must cycle between shapes or respond to varying loads.

      Potential impact

      Harnessing these transitions could lead to deployable structures that hold their shape, reusable energy absorbers and compliant mechanisms that switch in response to a physical stimulus.

      • Low-power reconfiguration
      • Reusable energy absorption
      • Mechanical switching and memory
