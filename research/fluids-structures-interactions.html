Research theme

      Fluid–Structure Interactions

      Vision

      When air or water moves past a slender structure, the flow can bend it and set it in motion. That motion in turn changes the flow and the forces acting on the structure. We study how this feedback develops and which responses it produces.

      We use experiments and models to relate a structure’s shape, stiffness and motion to the flow around it. Flexible sheets and simplified models of natural structures provide ways to isolate mechanisms such as flutter and vortex-induced vibration.

      Why it matters

      In some systems, an oscillation can amplify loads and accelerate fatigue. In others, a flexible structure can reorient under flow and reduce the load it carries. Small changes in flexibility or flow speed can move a system from one regime to another.

      Measuring when these transitions occur helps us understand both the limits and the opportunities of using compliant components in moving fluids.

      Potential impact

      The results can support vibration prediction and the design of surfaces that respond passively to aerodynamic or hydrodynamic loads.

      • Prediction of flow-induced vibration
      • Design of compliant structures in flow
      • Passive control of aerodynamic loads
