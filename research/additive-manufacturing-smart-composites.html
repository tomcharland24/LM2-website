Research theme

      Additive Manufacturing of Smart Composites

      Vision

      We explore how additive manufacturing can build sensing or actuation into a composite while the part is being made. The challenge is to arrange the reinforcement, matrix and functional materials so that they work together in the finished component.

      Our studies connect the deposition and processing of functional materials with mechanical and electrical measurements. On a curved part, for example, the deposition path determines where a sensor can be placed and how well it follows the surface.

      Why it matters

      Bonded or removable sensors can be difficult to position in enclosed areas or on tightly curved surfaces. Integrating them during fabrication can provide measurements where the loads actually pass through the structure.

      Integration also raises questions about adhesion, durability and calibration. A sensor needs to produce a repeatable signal through many loading cycles while the composite continues to carry its intended loads.

      Potential impact

      Combining structural and sensing functions could help engineers monitor strain or damage in service and produce instrumented components with fewer assembly steps.

      • In-situ structural monitoring
      • Conformal sensing on curved parts
      • Integrated multifunctional prototypes
