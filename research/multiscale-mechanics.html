      Multiscale Mechanics

      Vision

      A material can look uniform at the scale of a component and contain a complex distribution of fibres, interfaces and defects at smaller scales. We study how mechanical events at those scales determine deformation, damage and failure in the larger part.

      We connect local measurements and observations with numerical models. Measurements near a crack, for example, can reveal how deformation concentrates and provide a basis for checking whether a model predicts where the crack will extend.

      Why it matters

      A component test gives an overall measure of stiffness or strength. Local measurements help explain the mechanisms behind it, especially when several kinds of damage occur in the same material.

      Linking the two levels can also show when a property measured on a simple specimen is adequate for design and when the geometry or loading of the part must be represented explicitly.

      Potential impact

      Better links between scales could improve predictions of damage and service life and help engineers choose materials and processing routes for a given application.

      • Improved damage prediction
      • Better use of local measurements
      • Mechanically informed materials selection
