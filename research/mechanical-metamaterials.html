      Mechanical Metamaterials

      Vision

      Mechanical metamaterials use architecture to obtain a response at the scale of a whole part. We design the geometry of individual cells and their connections to control how the assembled structure bends, compresses or transmits loads.

      We combine simulation, fabrication and mechanical testing to examine how a proposed response changes when the cells are repeated or arranged in a pattern. This lets us test whether an effect predicted for a single cell persists across a specimen.

      Why it matters

      The geometry offers new choices when engineering requirements pull in different directions. A cellular panel can be designed to resist loading in one direction while deforming in a prescribed way in another.

      Manufacturing imperfections and interactions between neighbouring cells can change that response. Measuring these effects helps translate geometric designs into parts with behaviour that can be predicted and reproduced.

      Potential impact

      Architected materials could help tailor stiffness, manage impact energy or control the propagation of vibrations in a structure.

      • Tailored stiffness
      • Architectures for energy absorption
      • Programmable deformation
