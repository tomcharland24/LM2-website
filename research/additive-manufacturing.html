      Additive Manufacturing

      Vision

      Additive manufacturing lets us shape a part and its internal architecture at the same time. At LM2 we study how materials flow through a printhead, how adjacent deposits join, and how these steps determine the properties of the finished part.

      We combine controlled experiments with process models and robotic fabrication. A test can start with a single deposited bead and progress to a curved component, where tool orientation, cooling and deposition history all matter.

      Why it matters

      A part may look correct and still contain weak interfaces or voids. Two parts with the same external shape can behave differently under load because their layers cooled or bonded differently.

      Understanding these mechanisms gives us a way to choose process settings and toolpaths for the performance we need. It also helps establish which observations from a small specimen carry over to larger parts.

      Potential impact

      The goal is to make complex parts with predictable properties and to shorten the trial-and-error cycle when qualifying a new material, machine or geometry.

      • More consistent parts
      • Nonplanar deposition on complex surfaces
      • Better use of high-performance polymers
