      Deployable Structures Inspired by Origami and Kirigami

      Vision

      We use origami folds and kirigami cuts to design structures that transform from a compact package into a useful three-dimensional shape. The pattern determines how individual panels move and how those motions combine during deployment.

      We study which geometries favour a smooth transition, which lock into place, and how the choice of material affects both. Physical prototypes and simulations let us compare the intended motion with what happens under real loads.

      Why it matters

      Deployable systems face different demands in transit and in operation. A folded component must fit within a limited space, yet the deployed structure may need to hold its shape or withstand wind, pressure or mechanical loads.

      Geometry offers a way to coordinate these requirements. A well-chosen folding sequence can guide motion with fewer actuators and make the final shape less sensitive to disturbances during deployment.

      Potential impact

      These principles could support compact structures for transport or space, as well as adaptive surfaces that change their shape in response to the loads they encounter.

      • Compact stowage
      • Controlled deployment
      • Fabrication from flat stock
